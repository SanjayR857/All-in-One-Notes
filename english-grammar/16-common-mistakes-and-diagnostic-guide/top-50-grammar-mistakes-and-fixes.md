# Top 50 Grammar Mistakes & Fixes: The Bug-and-Patch Catalog 🩺🐛

> **Level:** CEFR A2–C1 (Comprehensive Error Remediation)  
> **Primary References:** Michael Swan (*Practical English Usage*, §31, 32–35); *Cambridge Learner Corpus*; Raymond Murphy (*English Grammar in Use*)  
> **Core Premise:** This catalog identifies the **fifty most frequent grammatical defects** observed in international engineering communication, technical documentation, code reviews, and workplace discourse. Each entry pairs the **broken antipattern (the bug)** with the **corrected idiomatic English (the patch)**, the underlying **grammatical invariant**, and a **diagnostic root-cause explanation**.

---

## 1. Verbs, Tenses & Auxiliaries (Bugs 1–10)

| # | ❌ Broken Anti-Pattern (Bug) | ✅ Corrected Idiomatic English (Patch) | Grammatical Invariant & Root Cause |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | ❌ *I am working here since three years.* | ✅ *I **have been working** here **for** three years.* | Ongoing duration originating in the past requires **Present Perfect Continuous** (`have been + V-ing`) + *for* (duration). |
| **2** | ❌ *A fatal error **was occurred** at midnight.* | ✅ *A fatal error **occurred** at midnight.* | *Occur* is strictly **intransitive** and cannot form a passive voice construction. |
| **3** | ❌ *Did you **went** to the standup meeting?* | ✅ *Did you **go** to the standup meeting?* | Auxiliary *did* already carries past tense; the lexical verb must remain in **bare infinitive** form (*go*). |
| **4** | ❌ *We **have deployed** the hotfix yesterday.* | ✅ *We **deployed** the hotfix yesterday.* | Definite past time adverbials (*yesterday, in 2024, ago*) mandate the **Past Simple**, forbidding the Present Perfect. |
| **5** | ❌ *He **said me** that the build failed.* | ✅ *He **told me** (or **said to me**) that the build failed.* | *Say* cannot take a direct personal recipient object without the preposition *to*; *tell* requires a personal object. |
| **6** | ❌ *The team suggested **to rewrite** the API.* | ✅ *The team suggested **rewriting** the API.* | *Suggest* strictly governs a **gerund** (`-ing`) or a *that*-clause, never a to-infinitive. |
| **7** | ❌ *I look forward to **hear** from you.* | ✅ *I look forward to **hearing** from you.* | In *look forward to*, `to` is a **preposition**, which universally demands a nominal gerund (`-ing`). |
| **8** | ❌ *The container **is stop** working.* | ✅ *The container **stopped / has stopped** working.* | Verbs cannot combine bare *be* with a bare verb; use past simple (*stopped*) or present perfect (*has stopped*). |
| **9** | ❌ *He **must to configure** the firewall.* | ✅ *He **must configure** the firewall.* | Core modal auxiliaries (*must, can, should, will*) govern **bare infinitives** without *to*. |
| **10**| ❌ *We **can able to** scale horizontally.* | ✅ *We **can** (or **are able to**) scale horizontally.* | Double modal redundancy: *can* and *be able to* express the same ability; never combine them. |

## 2. Subject-Verb Concord & Agreement (Bugs 11–20)

| # | ❌ Broken Anti-Pattern (Bug) | ✅ Corrected Idiomatic English (Patch) | Grammatical Invariant & Root Cause |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **11**| ❌ *The list of database servers **are** updated.* | ✅ *The list of database servers **is** updated.* | **Distance Attraction Error:** The head noun is singular *list*; intervening plural prepositional phrases do not alter agreement. |
| **12**| ❌ *Neither the lead nor the engineers **was** ready.* | ✅ *Neither the lead nor the engineers **were** ready.* | **Proximity Rule:** In *neither...nor*, the verb agrees with the closer subject (*engineers* $\rightarrow$ *were*). |
| **13**| ❌ *Everyone **have** their own development sandbox.* | ✅ *Everyone **has** their own development sandbox.* | Indefinite pronouns (*everyone, somebody, each*) are grammatically **singular** and take singular verbs. |
| **14**| ❌ *Each of the microservice endpoints **require** auth.*| ✅ *Each of the microservice endpoints **requires** auth.*| The subject is singular *Each*, which mandates a singular verb with 3rd-person `-s`. |
| **15**| ❌ *Telemetry data **shows** that CPU is high.* | ✅ *Telemetry data **shows / show** that CPU is high.*| *Data* is treated as singular mass noun in computing (*shows*), though plural (*show*) is accepted in formal academia. |
| **16**| ❌ *There **is** three critical bugs in this PR.* | ✅ *There **are** three critical bugs in this PR.* | In existential *there* clauses, the verb agrees with the delayed plural subject (*three critical bugs*). |
| **17**| ❌ *Ten thousand dollars **are** too expensive.* | ✅ *Ten thousand dollars **is** too expensive.* | Quantities of money, time, and distance functioning as a single lump-sum entity take a **singular verb**. |
| **18**| ❌ *The company **are** expanding its office.* | ✅ *The company **is** expanding **its** office.* | In American English and formal tech writing, corporations are singular collective entities. |
| **19**| ❌ *One of my colleagues **are** on call tonight.* | ✅ *One of my colleagues **is** on call tonight.* | The subject is singular *One*, requiring the singular copula *is*. |
| **20**| ❌ *Deploying containers **require** root privileges.* | ✅ *Deploying containers **requires** root privileges.* | A gerund phrase (*Deploying containers*) functions as a singular nominal subject. |

## 3. Nouns, Articles & Quantifiers (Bugs 21–30)

| # | ❌ Broken Anti-Pattern (Bug) | ✅ Corrected Idiomatic English (Patch) | Grammatical Invariant & Root Cause |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **21**| ❌ *We downloaded **a software** to test the API.* | ✅ *We downloaded **a software tool / some software**...*| *Software* is strictly **uncountable**; it cannot take indefinite article *a* or plural *-s*. |
| **22**| ❌ *Could you give me some **informations**?* | ✅ *Could you give me some **information**?* | *Information* is an uncountable mass noun in English; the plural form *informations* does not exist. |
| **23**| ❌ *We purchased new **equipments** for the lab.* | ✅ *We purchased new **equipment / pieces of equipment**.*| *Equipment* is an uncountable mass noun; it cannot take plural *-s*. |
| **24**| ❌ *We have **few** bugs left, so we can release.* | ✅ *We have **a few** bugs left, so we can release.* | *A few* = positive small number (some); *few* = negative near-zero shortage (*few bugs left, so we cannot release*). |
| **25**| ❌ *The server has **little** free memory.* | ✅ *The server has **little** free memory.* (Correct for near-zero; contrast: *a little* = some). | *Little* without *a* emphasizes scarcity/exhaustion of uncountable resources. |
| **26**| ❌ *He works as **software engineer**.* | ✅ *He works as **a** software engineer.* | Singular countable professions strictly demand an indefinite article (*a / an*). |
| **27**| ❌ *We deployed code to **the production**.* | ✅ *We deployed code to **production**.* | Standard engineering environments (*production, staging, master*) function as bare institutional proper locations. |
| **28**| ❌ *Open source software improves **the life**.* | ✅ *Open source software improves **life**.* | General abstract concepts take the **zero article**, not the definite article *the*. |
| **29**| ❌ *I have **too much** tasks to finish today.* | ✅ *I have **too many** tasks to finish today.* | Plural countable nouns (*tasks*) require *many*; *much* is reserved strictly for uncountables (*much work*). |
| **30**| ❌ *The system handles **less** requests than before.*| ✅ *The system handles **fewer** requests than before.* | Countable plural nouns (*requests*) take *fewer*; uncountable mass nouns take *less* (*less traffic*). |

## 4. Pronouns, Adjectives & Adverbs (Bugs 31–38)

| # | ❌ Broken Anti-Pattern (Bug) | ✅ Corrected Idiomatic English (Patch) | Grammatical Invariant & Root Cause |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **31**| ❌ *This decision is between you and **I**.* | ✅ *This decision is between you and **me**.* | Prepositions (*between*) govern the **objective pronoun case** (*me, him, them*), never the subjective (*I*). |
| **32**| ❌ *Our new server is **more better** than the old one.*| ✅ *Our new server is **much better** than the old one.*| Double comparative error: *better* is already comparative; modify with degree adverb *much*. |
| **33**| ❌ *The script executes **fastly**.* | ✅ *The script executes **fast** (or **rapidly**).* | *Fast* is both an adjective and an adverb; the word *fastly* does not exist in standard English. |
| **34**| ❌ *The engineer **hardly works**.* | ✅ *The engineer **works hard**.* | *Hardly* is a negative adverb meaning *almost not at all*; *hard* is the adverb of intense effort. |
| **35**| ❌ *He is the engineer **which** wrote the compiler.* | ✅ *He is the engineer **who** wrote the compiler.* | Human antecedents require the relative pronoun *who* or *that*, never *which*. |
| **36**| ❌ *The server lost **it's** network connection.* | ✅ *The server lost **its** network connection.* | Possessive determiner is **its** (no apostrophe); **it's** is strictly a contraction of *it is* or *it has*. |
| **37**| ❌ *Developers must test **theirselves** code.* | ✅ *Developers must test **their own** code.* | Reflexive pronoun is *themselves*; possessive emphatic is *their own*. |
| **38**| ❌ *He explained the concept **very clearly to us**.* | ✅ *(Correct word order; contrast: ❌ He explained us the concept).* | *Explain* cannot take an indirect object directly; use *explain something to someone*. |

## 5. Conditionals & Modality (Bugs 39–44)

| # | ❌ Broken Anti-Pattern (Bug) | ✅ Corrected Idiomatic English (Patch) | Grammatical Invariant & Root Cause |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **39**| ❌ *If it **will rain**, we will cancel the trip.* | ✅ *If it **rains**, we will cancel the trip.* | Future time clauses after *if, when, after, before* mandate the **Present Simple**, not *will*. |
| **40**| ❌ *If I **would have known**, I would have intervened.*| ✅ *If I **had known**, I would have intervened.*| Third conditional *if*-clauses strictly take `had + V3`, never *would have*. |
| **41**| ❌ *I wish I **am** in the meeting yesterday.* | ✅ *I wish I **had been** in the meeting yesterday.* | Past unreal wishes require the **Past Perfect** (*had been*). |
| **42**| ❌ *The system **should to** validate inputs.* | ✅ *The system **should** validate inputs.* | Modal *should* governs a bare infinitive without *to*. |
| **43**| ❌ *Unless you **don't** authenticate, access is denied.*| ✅ *Unless you **authenticate**, access is denied.* | *Unless* already carries negative meaning (*if not*); adding *don't* creates a double negative. |
| **44**| ❌ *I **could able to** fix the bug.* | ✅ *I **was able to / could** fix the bug.* | Single ability expression: use *was able to* for specific past accomplishments. |

## 6. Prepositions, Complementation & Questions (Bugs 45–50)

| # | ❌ Broken Anti-Pattern (Bug) | ✅ Corrected Idiomatic English (Patch) | Grammatical Invariant & Root Cause |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **45**| ❌ *We need to **discuss about** the architecture.* | ✅ *We need to **discuss** the architecture.* | *Discuss* is a transitive verb taking a direct object; adding *about* is an ungrammatical redundancy. |
| **46**| ❌ *The client must **comply to** RFC 2119.* | ✅ *The client must **comply with** RFC 2119.* | The verb *comply* governs the dependent preposition **with**, never *to*. |
| **47**| ❌ *Could you tell me **where is the server**?* | ✅ *Could you tell me **where the server is**?* | Embedded indirect questions prohibit subject-auxiliary inversion; order is `Subject + Verb`. |
| **48**| ❌ *He is married **with** an engineer.* | ✅ *He is married **to** an engineer.* | Marital relationship takes the preposition **to**, not *with*. |
| **49**| ❌ *The microservice depends **from** PostgreSQL.* | ✅ *The microservice depends **on / upon** PostgreSQL.* | The verb *depend* strictly takes the dependent preposition **on**, never *from*. |
| **50**| ❌ *They stopped **to deploy** because of an outage.* | ✅ *They stopped **deploying** because of an outage.* | Cessation of an ongoing activity requires the **gerund** (`-ing`), not the to-infinitive of purpose. |

## 7. Self-Assessment Practice Exercises

### Exercise 1: Diagnostic Error Identification
Identify and correct the single grammatical bug in each sentence:
1. *The DevOps team has deployed five new softwares to improve pipeline observability.*
2. *Neither the database administrator nor the site reliability engineers was notified.*
3. *Could you please explain me how the authentication token is regenerated?*
4. *If the upstream service will fail, the circuit breaker trips immediately.*
5. *We look forward to deploy the new container cluster next Monday.*

### Exercise 2: Quick-Fire Fill-in-the-Blank
Select the correct word to patch each broken sentence:
6. A critical vulnerability (occurred / was occurred) during yesterday's penetration test.
7. We must ensure that the application handles (fewer / less) concurrent database deadlocks.
8. This architectural decision was agreed upon between Sarah and (I / me).
9. The cluster lost (its / it's) connection to the primary storage volume.
10. If the security team (had audited / would have audited) the codebase, the flaw would have been prevented.

## 8. Answer Key with Diagnostic Explanations

1. **Correction:** *The DevOps team has deployed five new **software tools (or applications)**...*  
   *Explanation:* *Software* is uncountable; pluralize with countable noun counters (*tools/applications*).
2. **Correction:** *Neither the database administrator nor the site reliability engineers **were** notified.*  
   *Explanation:* Proximity concord with plural *engineers* demands plural verb *were*.
3. **Correction:** *Could you please explain **to me** how the authentication token is regenerated?*  
   *Explanation:* *Explain* cannot take a direct personal object (*explain me*); requires *explain to me*.
4. **Correction:** *If the upstream service **fails**, the circuit breaker trips immediately.*  
   *Explanation:* Conditional *if*-clause requires Present Simple (*fails*), not future *will fail*.
5. **Correction:** *We look forward to **deploying** the new container cluster next Monday.*  
   *Explanation:* Preposition *to* in *look forward to* strictly requires a gerund (`-ing`).
6. **occurred** — Intransitive verb; passive *was occurred* is impossible.
7. **fewer** — Modifying countable plural noun *deadlocks*.
8. **me** — Objective case after preposition *between*.
9. **its** — Possessive determiner without apostrophe (*it's* = it is).
10. **had audited** — Third conditional *if*-clause takes `had + V3`.

## 9. High-Density Revision Flashcards

```
Q: What are the three most common uncountable noun traps in software engineering?
A: 1. 'Software' (NOT 'a software' or 'softwares' ❌ -> use 'some software' or 'software applications' ✅).
   2. 'Information' (NOT 'informations' ❌ -> use 'information' or 'pieces of information' ✅).
   3. 'Equipment' (NOT 'equipments' ❌ -> use 'equipment' or 'pieces of equipment' ✅).
```

```
Q: Why is 'discuss about the architecture' a grammatical error?
A: 'Discuss' is a TRANSITIVE verb that takes a direct object (e.g., 'discuss the architecture').
   Adding 'about' is redundant. (Contrast: 'talk ABOUT', 'speak ABOUT').
```

```
Q: What happens to word order in indirect questions like 'Do you know where...'?
A: Inversion is BANNED! Revert to normal Subject + Verb affirmative order:
   'Do you know WHERE THE KEY IS?' (NOT: 'where is the key').
```
